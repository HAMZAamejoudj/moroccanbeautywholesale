Rhassoul, also spelled *ghassoul*, is a natural clay mined in the Atlas Mountains of Morocco. It has been part of the hammam ritual for centuries, used to wash both skin and hair. Today it is a favourite of natural beauty shops and spas, because it is simple, versatile and easy to explain.

## What makes rhassoul different

Rhassoul comes from deposits in the Middle Atlas, in eastern Morocco, and is not found in the same form anywhere else. It is a mineral-rich clay, naturally high in magnesium and silica. On ingredient lists, it usually appears as **Moroccan Lava Clay**.

Its main quality is that it cleanses gently. Mixed with water, it forms a smooth paste that absorbs excess oil and impurities without leaving the skin feeling stripped, which is why it is used as both a face mask and a hair wash.

## How to use rhassoul

Rhassoul is sold dry and mixed just before use. Explaining these steps on your product page helps customers get good results:

1. Put one to two tablespoons of rhassoul in a bowl, ideally glass, wood or ceramic.
2. Add water or rose water little by little, mixing until you get a smooth paste.
3. **For the face:** apply a thin layer, leave for 5 to 10 minutes, and rinse before it dries completely.
4. **For hair:** apply a looser paste to damp hair and scalp, massage gently, leave a few minutes and rinse well.
5. **In the hammam:** use after black soap and the kessa glove, as the final cleansing step.

## Powder or raw flakes?

Rhassoul comes in two main forms:

- **Fine powder:** ready to mix, easy to use, best for retail and for customers new to clay.
- **Raw flakes or pieces:** the traditional form, softened in water before use. Popular with customers who want the most authentic product, and with hammams.

There is also a traditional **rhassoul with herbs**, prepared with plants and flowers. It has a richer scent and makes a good premium option.

## What to check when buying in bulk

- **Texture and colour:** good rhassoul powder is fine, even, and brown to grey-brown, without sand or grit.
- **Clean packaging:** clay absorbs moisture and smells, so it should arrive in sealed packaging.
- **Batch number:** every bag or jar should carry one, along with a best-before date.
- **Documents:** ask for the ingredient list for your labels, and a Safety Data Sheet (SDS) if your market or platform requires it.

## Formats that work

- **Small pouches or jars (100 to 250 g)** for retail and online shops.
- **Larger bags** for spas, hammams and salons that prepare treatments every day.
- **Single-use portions** for hammam kits and hotel amenities.

## Storage

Keep rhassoul dry and closed, away from humidity. Customers should never put water into the main jar; mix only the amount needed in a separate bowl.

## FAQ

### Is rhassoul the same as ghassoul?
Yes. Rhassoul and ghassoul are two spellings of the same Moroccan clay.

### Can rhassoul be used on oily and dry skin?
It is popular for oily and combination skin. For dry skin, customers can mix it with rose water and add a drop of argan oil, and keep the mask short.

### Can rhassoul replace shampoo?
Many people use it as an occasional natural hair wash. It doesn't foam like shampoo, so it helps to explain this on the label.

### What is the minimum order?
You can start from 50 pieces and combine rhassoul with black soap, kessa gloves and other products in the same order.
