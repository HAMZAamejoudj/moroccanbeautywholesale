Moroccan black soap, called *savon beldi* in Morocco, is the first step of the traditional hammam. It doesn't look like soap at all: it is a soft, dark brown paste with a texture close to thick honey. For spas, hammams and shops that sell Moroccan products, it is one of the easiest products to explain and one of the most reordered.

## What black soap is

Traditional black soap is made from olive oil, often with crushed olives, saponified with potassium. That is why it is soft instead of hard like a bar. On an ingredient list, you will usually see **Potassium Olivate**, sometimes with added oils or essential oils.

Its dark colour comes from the olives, not from charcoal. This is a common confusion with "African black soap", which is a different product made in West Africa.

## How it is used in the hammam

The ritual is simple, and explaining it to your customers helps them use the product well:

1. Warm up the skin with steam or a hot shower for a few minutes.
2. Apply a small amount of black soap on damp skin and leave it for about five minutes.
3. Rinse, then scrub the whole body with a **kessa glove**, using firm strokes.
4. Rinse again and follow with a moisturizer or a few drops of argan oil.

Used this way, black soap softens the skin so the kessa glove can remove dead skin cells more easily. Most people use it once a week.

## Versions to stock

Black soap comes in several versions. A good starting range has two or three:

- **Plain (natural):** the classic, with only a mild olive smell.
- **Eucalyptus:** the most popular in hammams, with a fresh scent.
- **With argan oil:** a softer feel, good for retail and gift sets.
- **Rose or orange blossom:** floral versions that work well in boutiques and hotels.

## Formats for each type of business

- **Small jars (around 100 to 250 g)** for retail shelves, online shops and hotel rooms.
- **Larger jars or tubs** for spas and hammams that use the soap in treatments every day.
- **Mini portions** for hammam kits and welcome gifts.

If you are not sure which sizes will sell, start with a mixed order of 50 pieces and adjust after the first month.

## Storage and handling

Black soap should be stored with the lid closed, away from water and heat. Water getting into the jar shortens its life, so recommend customers use a dry spoon or spatula rather than wet hands. Each jar should have a batch number and a best-before date.

## Selling tips

Black soap sells best when customers understand the ritual. A small card with the four steps above, or a short video on your product page, makes a real difference. Pairing it with a kessa glove in the same listing or on the same shelf usually increases the order value.

## FAQ

### Is Moroccan black soap the same as African black soap?
No. Moroccan black soap is a soft paste made from olive oil and olives. African black soap is a different product made in West Africa from plant ashes and oils.

### Can black soap be used on the face?
Many people use it on the face in small amounts, without scrubbing hard. Recommend a patch test first, especially for sensitive skin.

### What is the minimum order for black soap?
You can start from 50 pieces and mix black soap with other products, such as kessa gloves, rhassoul clay or argan oil, in the same order.
